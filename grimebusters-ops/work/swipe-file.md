## Superlative-open ASMR hold — found 2026-08-22 (format itself dates to ~2023, now genre-standard)
Source: SB Pressure Washing / Spencer (multi-platform pressure-washing creator, largest in
the niche — cited at 55M+ followers, 3B+ annual views across platforms). Also the default
pattern across #pressurewashing / #drivewaycleaning tags generally.
Structure: Cold open on the dirtiest surface in the job, camera static, no face — text
overlay is a superlative claim ("dirtiest driveway I've seen in a while," "this hasn't
been cleaned in years"). 1–2 sec hold, then cut straight to wand/surface cleaner making
first contact. No spoken intro. Runs 15–45 sec total, ASMR audio (water hiss, machine hum)
either bare or under low trending audio.
Why it works: The claim in the overlay text sets a bar ("dirtiest") the footage then has
to visibly clear in the first pass — viewers stay to check if the claim holds. Removing
a spoken intro means zero drop-off before the pattern-interrupt of dirt actually lifting.
Fits Grime Busters? Yes, structurally easy — Nicolas already shoots this kind of footage.
Change: this is not a discovery, it's the floor of the niche. Every competitor and every
hobbyist account already does it. Posting it gets you parity, not differentiation — use
it as a reliable base-rate format (viewers expect it, it won't flop) but don't treat a win
here as a trend call. It's saturated, not dying — it's load-bearing genre convention now,
like a jump cut. Do not confuse "this performed" with "this is working right now"; it has
worked for three years straight and will keep working because it's mechanically simple,
not because it's fresh.

## POV problem-ownership hook — found 2026-08-22
Source: Recurring pattern across TikTok/IG pressure-washing tags, phrased as "POV: you
finally pressure wash [thing] you've been ignoring all year" / "...that's been collecting
years of grime."
Structure: Text overlay written in second person, present tense, naming a specific
neglected surface (patio + pool deck, driveway, back porch) — not "before/after," but
"you, finally dealing with this." Footage then plays as a normal job reel underneath.
Why it works: Second-person phrasing recruits the viewer's own guilt/procrastination
("that's literally my patio") instead of describing the business's work. It's a caption
trick, not a shooting trick — same footage, different frame, and it's cheap to test
against a neutral caption on the same clip.
Fits Grime Busters? Yes, no shot-list change needed — this is purely a caption swap Tyler
can A/B against a plain description on otherwise-identical footage. Cheapest test in this
file.

## Process-over-polish / "post you working" — found 2026-08-22
Source: Aggregate 2026 Reels-trend reporting (Later.com trend roundup), consistent with
what's outperforming in home-services/trades content broadly this year.
Structure: Raw, unedited-feeling clips of the job mid-process — tools out, mess visible,
owner talking to themselves or narrating a decision — instead of a polished final
before/after reveal. Often intercut with the messy middle (draining tank, swapping
nozzle, hauling hose) rather than cutting straight to results.
Why it works: Feed saturation with clean before/after content has made "polished" read as
an ad. Unpolished process footage signals "this is really happening, not staged," which
buys trust faster than a clean reveal does — the mechanism is credibility, not
satisfaction.
Fits Grime Busters? Yes, and it's cheap: don't reshoot, just stop cutting the messy middle
out of the edit. Needs testing against GB's own before/after posts to see if it actually
outperforms locally — no GB data exists yet to confirm (content-log.md is empty).
